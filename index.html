Under the hood

Real fluid physics,
running in your browser.

Four ping-pong GPU passes per frame. The tracers ride the same velocity field the pressure solve projects — so what you see is what the force integral feels.

01

Draw a silhouette

Sculpt directly in the test section, or start from an airfoil, cylinder or flat plate. Solid cells are rasterized into the solver mask at grid resolution.

02

Move the air

Semi-Lagrangian advection, divergence, Jacobi pressure iterations and a projection step run in ping-pong float framebuffers — every frame, on the GPU.

03

Measure the force

Pressure is read back asynchronously and integrated around the body boundary. Cd, Cl, drag and lift update at 20 Hz while the tunnel renders at 60.

How the wind tunnel works

01

Ping-pong GPU buffers

WebGL2 cannot read and write the same texture in one draw call. Velocity and pressure therefore live in two framebuffers. A pass reads read and renders into write; then the pair is swapped. This is the GPU-compute equivalent of a double buffer and avoids read-after-write hazards.

02

Fractional-step Navier–Stokes

Each frame: semi-Lagrangian advection traces velocity backwards in time, central differences compute divergence, 32–44 Jacobi iterations solve ∇²p = ∇·u, and the pressure gradient is subtracted from velocity. Solid cells enforce no-slip; the inlet carries a small time-varying shear.

03

65,536 GPU tracers

Particle positions are stored in a 256×256 RGBA32F texture. A fragment pass advances each tracer with midpoint RK2, respawning runaways at the inlet. The draw shader maps speed to deep blue → cyan → cream, with muted violet recirculation.

04

3D mode · D3Q19 lattice-Boltzmann

The 3D tunnel runs a D3Q19 BGK lattice-Boltzmann solver on a 72×36×36 grid with half-way bounce-back walls. Drag and lift come from momentum exchange on every solid link. Your 2D silhouette is lifted into a finite wing, a wall-to-wall extrusion, or a body of revolution. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, double-click to reset.

05

Camera capture

Pick an object colour (or the backdrop) and the webcam is thresholded in hue-aware colour space, smoothed over time, reduced to the largest blob, hole-filled and fitted to a quarter of the tunnel height — then used as the solid boundary. Freeze it to run a fair test.

06

Pressure force integration

A pixel-pack buffer reads the pressure field asynchronously. The CPU walks the solid/fluid interface, sums −p·n·ds into drag and lift, then normalizes to Cd and Cl against the body's reference length. UI metrics update at 20 Hz independently of the 60 FPS render loop.
